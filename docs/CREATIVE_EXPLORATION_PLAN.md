# GENESIS — Creative Exploration Plan

**Status:** Proposed working plan  
**Current stage:** Phase 0 — Foundation & pre-production  
**Creative partner:** Muse AI (concept exploration)  
**Project owner:** Human review and final decisions

## 1. Purpose

Explore GENESIS's visual identity and gameplay ideas before committing to production art. Muse is used as a creative exploration partner: generate alternatives, discover unexpected directions, refine selected concepts, and record useful ideas.

Concept images are references, not automatically approved designs or production-ready game assets.

## 2. Two parallel tracks

### Creative track — Muse AI + project owner

- Explore character silhouettes and evolution stages.
- Explore organic environments, regions, materials, lighting and palettes.
- Explore friendly, neutral and hostile organisms, predators and bosses.
- Explore UI, visual effects and evolution transformations when useful.
- Record prompts, variations, selected images, questions and provenance in `creative/muse/`.

### Design track — project owner + ChatGPT

- Evaluate concepts against the game pillars and player experience.
- Translate promising visuals into movement, traversal, combat, exploration or narrative opportunities.
- Identify production cost and solo-development risks.
- Keep decisions classified as Confirmed, Proposed, Open or Risk.
- Update the official design documents only after discussion and approval.

The tracks can influence each other. A new image may inspire a mechanic, but it does not silently become a requirement.

## 3. Exploration sequence

| Order | Focus | Main question | Expected output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Visual identity | What makes GENESIS recognizable at a glance? | Moodboards, palette and material studies |
| 2 | Character evolution | How does one protagonist transform while remaining recognizable? | Evolution lineup and silhouette studies |
| 3 | First environment | What does the first playable region feel and look like? | Environment concepts and visual landmarks |
| 4 | Creatures | How do organisms communicate their role and behavior visually? | Creature sheets and behavior notes |
| 5 | Evolution moments | How can transformation feel powerful and readable? | Transformation keyframes / VFX concepts |
| 6 | UI and feedback | How can information feel biological without hurting readability? | HUD and evolution-screen explorations |

This is a flexible order, not a deadline. Start with whichever subject is inspiring.

## 4. Review criteria

For each promising concept, review:

1. **Identity:** Does it feel like GENESIS rather than a direct imitation of another game?
2. **Readability:** Can the player understand its shape, role, threat and interaction?
3. **Gameplay value:** Does it suggest a useful mechanic, traversal option, encounter or story beat?
4. **Consistency:** Does it belong to the same world and visual language?
5. **Production cost:** Can a solo developer create, animate and maintain it?
6. **Emotional value:** Does it reinforce wonder, vulnerability, growth or the parental theme where appropriate?

Do not optimize every image for all six criteria. Some explorations exist only to discover a direction.

## 5. Concept workflow

1. **Explore:** Generate multiple distinct directions; avoid polishing the first result too early.
2. **Compare:** Select concepts for identity, clarity, gameplay potential and feasibility.
3. **Refine:** Iterate on silhouette, proportions, palette, materials and details.
4. **Design review:** Discuss implications with the project owner.
5. **Record:** Save the selected image, prompt and a short concept note.
6. **Promote:** Only approved decisions are copied into the official design documents.

## 6. Status labels

- **Exploration:** Raw experiment; no commitment.
- **Shortlisted:** Worth further iteration.
- **Candidate:** Strong option awaiting design approval.
- **Approved direction:** Accepted as the current art/design direction.
- **Superseded:** Retained for history, no longer preferred.

An approved direction is still not necessarily a production-ready asset.

## 7. Scope guard

- Prefer a few distinctive, reusable visual ideas over a large volume of disconnected concepts.
- Do not design every region, enemy or evolution branch before the first playable slice is validated.
- Avoid detailed animation sheets until silhouette, gameplay purpose and art direction are stable.
- Prefer concepts that can be simplified into a repeatable 2D production workflow.
- Keep the initial game target at 4–6 hours as a proposed scope; do not add content solely because concept generation is inexpensive.

## 8. First creative milestone

The first milestone is complete when we have:

- A small set of visual identity directions to compare.
- A protagonist evolution lineup with recognizable continuity.
- Two or three first-region environment directions.
- A handful of creature concepts with gameplay-role hypotheses.
- A short review identifying what is promising, what conflicts, and what needs another iteration.

No code or final asset production is required for this milestone.
